The moment a mechanic hands you a four-figure estimate, the instinct is to ask "who will give me a **car repair loan with bad credit in Canada?**" — and start applying. Slow down for ten minutes, because that instinct skips the two questions that decide whether you save money or lose it: *is this car even worth fixing*, and *what is the cheapest safe way to pay if it is?* Answer those first and the loan becomes a tool. Skip them and you can finance a repair on a car that was never worth the repair. This is the decision-first guide.

## Quick Answer

Yes, you can get a car repair loan with bad credit — income-based and secured lenders will work with a low score if the payment fits your budget, all capped at **35% APR**. But borrow in the right order: **(1)** decide whether to repair or replace using the "half the car's value" rule of thumb, **(2)** never commit at the tow yard — get a written second quote, and **(3)** fund it cheapest-first, from a garage plan or credit union down to an income-based installment loan, with payday and car-title loans as the last resort. When you have decided borrowing is right, you can compare options on our [loans overview](/loans) and [start an application](/loan-application) in minutes — no judgment about your credit history.

## Step 1: Should You Fix This Car at All?

Before a single loan application, do the repair-versus-replace math. It is the highest-value five minutes in this whole process, and it is the step the "apply now" articles skip.

Start with a simple, honest rule of thumb: **if the repair costs more than about half of what the car is currently worth — or more than roughly a year of payments on a reliable replacement — think hard before borrowing to fix it.** A $2,800 transmission on a car worth $3,000 is usually good money chasing bad. A $600 brake job on a car worth $9,000 is a clear fix.

Work through these quickly:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is the car realistically worth today? | Sets the ceiling on a sensible repair spend |
| What does the written estimate total? | Repair cost vs. value is the core ratio |
| Is more likely to break soon? | An old car with one big failure often has others |
| Is it safety-critical or cosmetic? | Changes your urgency, not always your decision |
| What would a reliable replacement cost monthly? | The real alternative to the repair |

If the numbers say repair, borrowing to keep a paid-off car on the road is often smarter than taking on a whole car payment — our [car repair financing guide](/resources/guides/car-repair) digs into that trade-off. If the numbers say replace, a repair loan is the wrong product, and you have just saved yourself from financing a lost cause.

## Step 2: Don't Decide at the Tow Yard

Car repairs are one of the few purchases made under genuine pressure — you are stranded, stressed, and the shop has all the leverage. That pressure is exactly when people overpay, so build in a pause.

- **Triage safety first.** If the problem is brakes, steering, tires, or anything that makes the car unsafe or illegal to drive, do not drive it — but you can still take a day to arrange funding rather than saying yes on the spot. If the issue is cosmetic or non-urgent, you have even more time to shop.
- **Get a written second quote.** Prices for the same job vary widely between shops. A written estimate lets you compare, spot padding, and negotiate — and lenders and you both benefit from an exact number rather than a rough "around two grand."
- **Separate the must-fix from the nice-to-fix.** A quote often bundles urgent work with recommended extras. Fund what keeps the car safe and legal now; defer the rest.

Ten minutes of not-deciding can knock hundreds off the bill — which shrinks the loan you need in the first place.

## Step 3: The Funding Ladder — Cheapest to Most Expensive

Once you know the real number, fund it from the top of this ladder down. Only move to the next rung when the one above genuinely is not available to you.

| Rung | Option | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Garage payment plan / genuine 0% shop financing | "Deferred interest" that back-charges if not paid in full by the deadline |
| 2 | Credit-union small loan | Membership may be required; often the best rate for bad credit |
| 3 | Existing credit card (if the rate is reasonable) | Only if you can clear it fast; watch the APR |
| 4 | Income-based installment loan | Confirm it reports to the bureaus; keep it under 35% APR |
| 5 | Payday or car-title loan | Highest cost and highest risk — last resort only |

A few notes that save real money. On rung one, a **true 0% shop offer** is great, but "same as cash" and **deferred-interest** deals can back-charge every dollar of interest from day one if you miss the payoff date — read the fine print. A **credit union** (rung two) is often the quietly best deal for a borrower with bad credit and worth a call before anything else. And an **income-based installment loan** (rung four) is the workhorse for most bad-credit repair borrowers, because these lenders judge affordability over score — see how they compare with short-term options in our [payday vs personal loans](/resources/comparisons/payday-vs-personal-loans) breakdown, and browse [car-repair loan options](/loans/by-purpose/car-repairs) built for this.

## Getting a Fair Car Repair Loan With Bad Credit

If an installment loan is your rung, a few moves improve both your odds and your rate — bad credit or not.

- **Show steady, verifiable income.** Income-based lenders care more about regular deposits and job stability than your score. Our guide to your [debt-to-income ratio](/resources/guides/understanding-debt-to-income-ratio) shows the affordability math they run.
- **Right-size the request.** Borrow the exact repair total, not a round number above it. A smaller, clearly affordable payment reads as lower risk — compare realistic amounts like a [$1,500 loan](/loans/by-amount/1500) against your written quote.
- **Consider a co-signer** for a larger or safer repair loan, if someone creditworthy will back you and understands the responsibility.
- **Be realistic about "same-day."** Fast decisions are common; fast *funding* depends on the lender, verification and your bank. Treat same-day money as a possibility with conditions, not a guarantee.

## The Traps: Title Loans, Payday Loans, and the 35% Cap

Two products get pushed hard at stranded drivers, and both deserve caution.

A **car-title (logbook) loan** is secured against the very vehicle you are trying to keep running. Fall behind and you can lose the car — defeating the entire purpose of the repair — and the costs are steep. A **payday loan** is built for a small, two-week gap, not a repair bill; its short term and balloon repayment can trap you in a cycle, and there are almost always cheaper routes for a few hundred dollars. If you are tempted by either, first read our guide to [payday-loan alternatives](/resources/guides/payday-loan-alternatives) and weigh the collateral risk in our [secured vs unsecured loans](/resources/comparisons/secured-vs-unsecured-loans) comparison.

Whatever you choose, one hard line protects you: as of **January 1, 2025**, Canada's criminal rate of interest is capped at **35% APR**. Any standard installment loan above that is illegal. Ignore any **"guaranteed approval, no credit check"** pitch, never pay an **upfront fee** to release a loan, and confirm the APR in writing. Our guide on [spotting predatory lenders](/resources/guides/how-to-spot-predatory-lenders) and the federal [FCAC page on personal loans](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/personal-loans.html) show what a fair deal looks like.
