The problem with almost every **cash money review** published in 2026 is not bias — it is age. This brand changed more between 2025 and 2026 than in the previous decade: a renamed parent, a regulator-ordered refund, an exit from payday lending in one province, and a switch to online-only. Reviews written before those events describe a company that no longer operates the way they say it does.

## Quick answer

Five dated facts define this brand right now: the **$14 per $100** federal cap (January 1, 2025), the **CURO → Attain Finance** rebrand (February 5, 2025), a **$547,000 refund** to 2,800+ B.C. consumers (September 15, 2025), the **end of payday lending in B.C.** with stores converted to LendDirect, and the completion of a shift to **online-only payday lending** (March 26, 2026). Use them to date any **cash money review** you read — including this one.

## Change 1: the parent company has a different name

Cash Money is a Canadian brand of **Attain Finance**, which operated as **CURO Group Holdings** until it rebranded on **February 5, 2025**. Attain's other brands include **LendDirect** in Canada and **Heights Finance** in the United States.

This is a small fact with a big diagnostic use: a review still describing "CURO" as the parent was written before February 2025 and has not been touched since.

## Change 2: a regulator ordered $547,000 in refunds

This is the most substantive finding in this **cash money review**, and it is almost entirely absent from the pages currently ranking for the term.

On **September 15, 2025**, Consumer Protection BC announced that Cash Money had agreed to refund **$547,000 to more than 2,800 consumers**, covering more than **5,600 non-compliant payday loans** issued between **June 1, 2023 and July 20, 2025**. The regulator alleged the company had *"violated consumer protection laws in their loan agreements by asking for payment before borrowers had the funds from their employers or other sources"* and, in some cases, gave borrowers a **shorter repayment timeframe than the law requires**. Cash Money entered a compliance agreement to correct its contracts and mailed refund cheques.

**If you took a payday loan from this company in British Columbia in that window, you may be owed money.** Consumer Protection BC published the company's contact details for refund eligibility: **1-877-526-6639** and **customerservice@cashmoney.ca**.

The wider lesson is bigger than one brand: a fully licensed, well-reviewed, decades-old lender ran non-compliant contracts for roughly two years. No star rating detected it. A regulator's inspection did.

## Change 3 this cash money review tracks: no payday loans in B.C.

Following the settlement, reporting stated that Cash Money **stopped offering payday loans in British Columbia** and converted its retail locations there to its **LendDirect** affiliate, which offers instalment loans rather than payday loans.

That single change invalidates a large share of what is currently published about the brand. Reviews listing "Consumer Protection BC" among its active payday licences, or directing B.C. readers to a Cash Money payday application, are describing arrangements that ended.

## Change 4: payday lending moved online only

On **March 26, 2026**, Attain Finance announced it had completed the strategic repositioning of its Canadian business. In the company's own words: *"The multi-year endeavor of going cashless while converting its payday business to online only, under Cash Money, is complete."* Canadian operations now focus on the near-prime omni-channel business under the **LendDirect** brand, offering lines of credit and secured loans. Chief executive **Doug Clark** described it as "moving single pay customers to digital."

So the practical test for any review of this brand: **if it tells you to visit a branch for a payday loan, it is out of date.**

## Change 5: the price ceiling changed, and stale reviews still show it

Since **January 1, 2025**, a licensed payday loan in Canada is capped at **$14 per $100** advanced, all-in, under the federal Criminal Interest Rate Regulations. Pre-2025 provincial caps of **$15** and **$17** no longer apply to new agreements.

At the time of writing, pages ranking on the first page for this keyword still describe costs of "$14 and $15 per $100." That is a reliable staleness signal, and it travels: a page stale on the headline price is usually stale on licensing, channel and product range too.

## If you borrowed in B.C. between June 2023 and July 2025

This is the most actionable section of this cash money review, and it applies to a specific group of people who may not know it.

Consumer Protection BC's settlement covered payday loans issued in British Columbia between **June 1, 2023 and July 20, 2025**. Refund cheques were mailed — but mailed cheques go to the address on file, and people who moved, changed banks or simply did not open the envelope can easily have missed one.

**How to check:**

1. Find any record of a payday loan from that lender in B.C. in that window — a bank statement line, an email confirmation, or the agreement itself.
2. Contact the company using the details the regulator published: **1-877-526-6639** or **customerservice@cashmoney.ca**.
3. Ask specifically whether your loans were among the non-compliant agreements identified in the Consumer Protection BC compliance agreement, and whether a refund was issued to you.
4. If you moved during that period, say so — an address change is the likeliest reason a cheque never arrived.
5. If you cannot get a clear answer, contact Consumer Protection BC.

There is no cost to asking. The refund pool was **$547,000** across more than **2,800 consumers**, which averages roughly **$195 per person** — worth a phone call for most households, and worth considerably more to some.

It is also a reminder that consumer-protection outcomes only reach the people who notice them. Regulators publish; almost nobody reads the publication.

## Your protections, whichever lender you use

**$14 per $100** maximum cost of borrowing; a loan of no more than **50% of net income** in Ontario; **two business days** to cancel in Ontario and B.C. and a **48-hour clock** in Manitoba; no rollovers; one payday loan at a time; and a mandatory **extended payment plan** on a third loan within 63 days in Ontario.

Default charges sit *outside* the 14% cap — in Ontario, a **$20** maximum dishonoured payment fee and **2.5% per month**, non-compounding, on outstanding principal, plus your bank's NSF fee, capped at **$10** federally since March 12, 2026.

The most useful thing a **cash money review** can give you in 2026 is not a score — it is a set of dates. Check the dates on whatever you read. Check the licence register for your province on the day you apply. And if you borrowed in B.C. between June 2023 and July 2025, check whether you are owed a refund.
