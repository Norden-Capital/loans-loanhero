**Do payday loans go on credit report Canada** files at all? For most borrowers who repay on schedule, the honest answer is no — nothing appears anywhere. The loan is invisible to Equifax and TransUnion from beginning to end. That sounds like good news until you notice the asymmetry underneath it: the silence only holds while things go well. This guide covers exactly what shows up, when, under whose name, and for how long.

## Quick Answer

A repaid payday loan usually leaves no trace, because reporting is voluntary in Canada and most payday lenders opt out. A defaulted one usually does appear — but as a **collection account reported by an agency**, not as a loan reported by the lender. It then sits on your file for six years from first delinquency, regardless of whether you pay it.

## Do Payday Loans Go on Credit Report Canada Files? The Asymmetry

The reason **do payday loans go on credit report Canada** searches get contradictory answers is that reporting is optional. There is no law in Canada, federal or provincial, requiring a payday lender to report borrowing or repayment data to the credit bureaus. Reporting is voluntary and most short-term lenders decline, because the data has value to competitors and reporting carries compliance obligations.

The result is a one-way valve:

| What you do | What reaches your credit file |
|---|---|
| Borrow and repay 12 loans on time | Usually **nothing** |
| Borrow and repay early | Usually **nothing** |
| Default once | A **collection account**, typically within a few months |

This is the single most important thing to understand about payday borrowing and credit. It is not a product that can build a file. It is a product that can only damage one. If building credit is your goal, a credit-builder loan or a secured card does the job that a payday loan cannot.

## Why the Entry Carries a Name You Don't Recognise

When a payday loan defaults, the lender typically assigns or sells the debt to a collection agency. The agency is the party with a bureau reporting relationship, so the tradeline that appears on your file carries **the agency's name**, not the lender's.

This is why so many people pull their report, see a collection from a company they have never dealt with, and assume it is an error. Sometimes it is. Often it is the payday loan, wearing a different name.

You are entitled to ask, in writing, for the identity of the original creditor and the details of the debt. In Ontario, before contacting you at all, a collection agency must first send written notice naming the creditor, stating the amount, identifying the agency and including the government's "Debt Collection: Information About Your Rights" statement — then wait six calendar days. If you never received that notice, say so.

## How Long Do Payday Loans Go on Credit Report Canada Files For?

Here the published rule and the actual practice differ, and almost every article quotes only one of them.

**The law:** Ontario's Consumer Reporting Act permits a consumer reporting agency to include a debt where no payment has been made for up to **seven years** from the default or the matter giving rise to the collection.

**The practice:** Equifax Canada and TransUnion Canada both apply a **six-year purge in every province**, measured from the date of first delinquency.

So the six-year figure you see everywhere is the bureaus' own policy, not a statutory maximum — and it is shorter than Ontario law would allow. Worth knowing if you are ever told an entry must stay for seven years.

## How to Read the Entry When You Find It

If something has appeared, the entry itself tells you more than the score does. Four fields carry the weight:

- **The rating.** Canadian files use an R-scale from R1 to R9. R1 means paid as agreed; **R9** is the worst rating, used for a debt sent to collections or written off. A payday-related entry almost always arrives as an R9 rather than progressing through the middle values, because it skips the "late but paying" stage entirely.
- **Date opened.** On a collection tradeline this is often the date the *agency* took the file, not the date you borrowed. It is not the date that controls the six years.
- **Date of last activity.** This is the field that matters, and it is where a small payment can have an effect. Check it against your own records.
- **Balance vs original amount.** A gap between them tells you what has been added since default — the place where charges outside the cost cap accumulate.

If any of those four look wrong, that is a dispute, and it is free to file with both bureaus. Whether payday loans go on credit report Canada files correctly is worth checking rather than assuming; errors in dates and amounts on sold debt are common, and a wrong date of first delinquency can leave an entry sitting on your file a year longer than it should.

## Two Clocks, and Why Paying Can Restart One

A defaulted payday loan runs two separate timers, and they behave differently:

| | Credit reporting clock | Limitation clock |
|---|---|---|
| **Length** | 6 years (bureau policy) | 2 years in most provinces |
| **Starts from** | Date of first delinquency | Your last payment or written acknowledgment |
| **Effect of a small payment** | Does not extend the six years | **Can restart the two years** |
| **What it controls** | How long your score is affected | Whether you can still be sued |

The practical warning: a $20 goodwill payment on a four-year-old collection does not shorten its time on your report, but it may hand the creditor a fresh two-year window to sue. If a debt is old, get advice before paying anything toward it.

## What to Do Right Now

1. **Pull both reports free.** Equifax and TransUnion each provide a free consumer disclosure. Payday-related entries frequently appear on one and not the other.
2. **Match every collection to a real debt.** Ask in writing for the original creditor's name if anything is unfamiliar.
3. **Dispute what is wrong**, in writing, with the bureau and the furnisher. Errors in amounts and dates are common.
4. **Check the first delinquency date** on any collection — that, not the date the agency acquired the file, sets the six-year expiry.
5. **Build something positive alongside it.** A negative entry ages out; an empty file does not improve on its own.

## Where This Leaves Payday Borrowing

So, **do payday loans go on credit report Canada** files often enough to matter? The reporting asymmetry means the answer is effectively no when you repay and yes when you don't — which is the worst possible shape for a credit-building strategy.

If you need short-term money, price it honestly: the federal cap since 1 January 2025 is 14% of the amount advanced, and a dishonoured-payment fee of up to $20 plus default interest of up to 2.5% per month sit outside that cap. If you also need your credit file to improve, that has to come from a different product entirely — our companion piece on [whether payday loans hurt your credit](/blog/are-payday-loans-bad-for-credit) covers the score mechanics, and our [credit report guide](/resources/guides/understanding-credit-reports) explains how to read what you find.

## The Bottom Line

Repay a payday loan and your credit file will most likely never know it existed. Default and it will — as a collection, under an agency's name, for six years from the first missed payment, whether or not you eventually pay. Pull both reports, verify the delinquency date, and be careful about making small payments on old debts.
