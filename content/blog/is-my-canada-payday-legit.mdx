**Is My Canada Payday legit** is one of the most-searched brand questions in Canadian short-term lending — and the honest answer is that no article, including this one, should be your source of truth for it. Licence status is a live record that changes, and it differs province by province. What an article *can* do is show you exactly how to check it yourself in about four minutes, and point out the thing most people miss: with a recognisable lending brand, the greater risk is usually not the company. It is someone pretending to be the company.

## Start with the right question

"Is My Canada Payday legit?" and "is the thing in front of me actually My Canada Payday?" are different questions, and for a well-known brand the second one matters more.

Here is the tell that makes the point. My Canada Payday publishes a **scam-alerts page** on its own website. That page states that **Westrock Financial Ltd. will never ask for payment before disbursing a loan or for your online banking login information**, and it directs consumers to provincial consumer protection ministries to verify a lender's legitimacy.

Companies do not build pages like that for fun. They build them because their name, branding and licence details are being copied by operators who then contact people by text, WhatsApp, social media and lookalike domains. The victim performs the usual checks — the name is real, the licence number is real, the address is real — and every check passes, because every detail was borrowed from a genuine business.

So the framework below does two jobs: it answers is My Canada Payday legit from the regulator's records, and it verifies that what reached you actually came from the company rather than from someone wearing its name.

## The 6 checks that answer "is My Canada Payday legit" — or any named lender

### Check 1 — Search the legal name, not the brand

Anyone asking is My Canada Payday legit hits this first, because provincial registries list the **legal entity** rather than the brand. My Canada Payday publishes its consumer-safety material under the name **Westrock Financial Ltd.**, and that corporate-name-versus-brand-name split is completely normal in Canadian lending.

It also causes a great deal of unnecessary alarm. People search the brand, get no result, and conclude fraud. Others see a matching brand on a cloned site and conclude safety. Both errors come from searching the wrong string.

Find the legal name in the **website footer**, the **Terms of Use**, or the **Privacy Policy** — a compliant lender publishes it in all three — and search that.

### Check 2 — Use your own province's registry

A lender needs a licence where **you** live, not where it is based. An operator licensed in British Columbia is not thereby licensed to lend to you in Nova Scotia.

### Check 3 — Read the status field, not just the search hit

A registry result is not a pass. Licences lapse, get suspended, and get surrendered, and stale records remain visible.

Confirm the **status reads Active** and the **expiry date is in the future**. Do this on the day you apply, not from a memory of checking last year — this is precisely why a review article cannot substitute for the registry.

### Check 4 — Verify the channel that contacted you

This is the check that defeats impersonation, and it is the one people skip.

If a text message, email, DM or ad brought you here, **do not use its contact details**. Take the phone number from the registry record, or type the official domain directly into your browser. Then ask whether the offer is theirs.

Watch for lookalike domains — an added hyphen, a doubled letter, `.co` instead of `.ca`. Those are cheap to register and they are the standard delivery mechanism.

### Check 5 — Price it against $14 per $100

Since **January 1, 2025**, the federal Criminal Interest Rate Regulations have capped the total cost of borrowing on a payday loan at **14% of the amount advanced** — $14 per $100 — in every province with a licensed payday regime. That replaced the older provincial caps of $15 (Ontario, BC, Alberta, Nova Scotia, New Brunswick, PEI) and $17 (Manitoba, Saskatchewan).

On a $500 advance, the maximum legal cost is **$70**.

Two charges sit legitimately outside that cap: a dishonoured-payment fee of **$20 or less**, and default interest of up to **2.5% per month**. Anything else — an application fee, an insurance premium, a processing charge — does not fit inside the law.

### Check 6 — Confirm your province licenses payday lending

Nine provinces do. **Quebec and the three territories do not.** Because the federal exemption applies only in designated provinces, a payday-priced loan offered to a Quebec resident is not exempt from the 35% criminal interest rate.

If you are in Quebec and any lender — recognisable brand or not — is offering a two-week advance at payday pricing, the issue is not which company to pick.

## What the company's own guidance says when you ask is My Canada Payday legit

The scam-alerts material published under the Westrock Financial name is worth reading as a checklist, because it describes the boundary between the real operation and the fakes:

- **No payment before disbursement.** This aligns exactly with Ontario's rule that a licensed lender cannot request or require payment of any part of the cost of borrowing before the agreement's end date. Money reaches you first.
- **No online banking credentials.** Legitimate income verification runs through a read-only third-party portal — Flinks, Inverite or Plaid — where you authenticate with your own bank and the lender receives data, not your username and password.
- **Verify through provincial ministries.** The company points people to the regulator rather than asking them to trust its own website, which is the correct instruction.

Those three lines are a usable test for any lender, not just this one.

## Your rights once you sign — with any licensed lender

Answering is My Canada Payday legit is the start, not the end. Under Ontario's framework and its provincial equivalents you get:

- **A two-day cancellation right.** You may cancel a payday loan agreement without reason up to the end of the second day after the lender provides the agreement and advance — no penalty, no explanation required.
- **A first-page cost table** in plain language, with the cost expressed as an **APR**.
- **No rollovers.** A licensed lender cannot issue a new payday loan while you have an outstanding payday balance with them.
- **An extended payment plan** by the third agreement within 63 days — instalments rather than another lump sum.
- **Collection limits.** No more than three contacts per week, no contacting your family, friends or acquaintances, no repeatedly re-presenting a debit in a way that generates overdraft charges, and no threatening language.

And a genuinely significant protection if the lender turns out **not** to be licensed: under section 6(3) of Ontario's *Payday Loans Act, 2008*, a borrower who enters a payday loan agreement with an unlicensed lender **is only required to repay the advance and is not liable to pay the cost of borrowing.**

## Before you apply anywhere: 5 questions worth emailing

Whether you are asking is My Canada Payday legit or checking any other brand, send these and keep the replies. A licensed lender answers all five in a few lines.

1. **"What is your legal entity name and licence number in my province?"**
2. **"What is the total dollar cost on the amount I'm requesting?"** — a number, not a rate.
3. **"Will any payment be required from me before the funds arrive?"** — the answer must be no.
4. **"Are you the lender of record, or will my application go to other companies?"**
5. **"What happens if a payment fails?"** — the legitimate answer is a dishonoured-payment fee of $20 or less plus default interest capped at 2.5% per month.

That email thread costs nothing to create and is the most useful evidence you will have if anything goes wrong later.
