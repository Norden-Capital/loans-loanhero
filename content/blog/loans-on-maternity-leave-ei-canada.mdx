Most articles about **loans on maternity leave EI Canada** lenders offer stop at "yes, EI counts as income." That is true and nearly useless. What actually determines whether the loan helps or hurts is *how EI pays* — every two weeks, capped at **$729** in 2026, taxable, and under-withheld. Each of those four properties creates a specific, predictable problem. Here is what each one does and how to work around it.

*Last reviewed August 2, 2026. This is general educational information, not financial or tax advice. Confirm benefit rules with Service Canada and loan terms directly with any lender.*

## Fact 1: Your ceiling is $729 a week, and that sets your loan size

The 2026 numbers:

| Benefit | Rate | 2026 weekly maximum | Weeks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maternity | 55% of average insurable earnings | $729 | 15 |
| Parental — standard | 55% | $729 | up to 35 |
| Parental — extended | 33% | ~$438 | up to 61 |

Maximum insurable earnings are **$68,900**, so anyone earning above that receives the same $729 as someone earning exactly $68,900. That single cap is why **loans on maternity leave EI Canada** applicants qualify for are usually smaller than expected — the lender is underwriting a $37,900 annualized income, whatever your job pays.

If you chose the **extended** option, the ceiling drops to roughly $438 a week, and loan approvals shrink accordingly. Anyone planning to borrow during an 18-month leave should assume conservative approvals, because loans on maternity leave EI Canada lenders write are sized directly off that weekly figure.

## Fact 2: The bi-weekly cycle is what actually causes missed payments

This is the most practical thing in this article and it is almost never mentioned.

EI pays **every two weeks**. Rent, loans, insurance, daycare and utilities are **monthly**. Twenty-six deposits do not line up with twelve billing cycles, so the two schedules drift. Roughly twice a year, a month contains bills due before the next deposit lands — and that is when a pre-authorized debit fails.

Three fixes:

1. **Set every due date you control to two days after a scheduled EI deposit**, not to a fixed calendar day. Loan payment dates are usually negotiable at signing; ask.
2. **Map your deposit dates for the next six months** from My Service Canada Account and mark the two "long" months in advance.
3. **Keep a small buffer specifically for those two months** — $200 is usually enough.

Worth noting that since **March 12, 2026**, federal rules cap NSF fees at **$10** on personal accounts, down from as much as $50, with no more than one fee per two business days and none at all on shortfalls under $10. A bounced payment is much cheaper than it used to be — but the *lender's* late fee and the credit report entry are not covered by that cap, so timing still matters.

## Fact 3: The return-to-work letter is worth more than a perfect credit score

Lenders treat EI as temporary income, and temporary income supports smaller loans. A letter on employer letterhead confirming your job title, your salary on return, and a **confirmed return date** lets many lenders underwrite against your **pre-leave salary** instead of your benefit amount. A confirmed return within 12 months is a common threshold for using full income.

Ask HR for it before you apply anywhere. It is free, it takes a day, and it routinely moves an application from a small subprime offer to a mainstream one — the largest single swing available on loans on maternity leave EI Canada lenders assess.

## Fact 4: You will probably owe tax in April

EI benefits are taxable. Service Canada withholds income tax from each payment, but the withheld amount frequently falls short when EI is combined with employment income in the same calendar year — which is exactly what happens in the year your leave starts and the year it ends.

The result is a balance owing at filing, arriving right around the time you return to work and childcare costs begin. Many parents discover this the same month, and it is a common reason people search for **loans on maternity leave EI Canada** lenders offer *after* the leave rather than during it.

**The fix costs nothing:** set aside 5–10% of each EI deposit in a separate account from day one. If you end up not owing, you have a buffer. If you do owe, you are not borrowing at 35% to pay the CRA — and the CRA itself will set up a payment arrangement on request, at a far lower cost than any consumer loan.

## Fact 5: Working while on claim changes your deposits — and your bank scan

Under the Working While on Claim provision, earnings generally reduce your benefits by **50 cents for every dollar earned**, up to **90%** of the weekly earnings used to establish your claim, with amounts above that deducted dollar for dollar. Report earnings in the week the work was **performed**, not when you were paid. Maternity and parental benefits are treated differently, so confirm the rules for your specific benefit with Service Canada.

The borrowing angle: most alternative lenders decide by scanning 90 days of your bank transactions. Part-time earnings plus reduced EI creates a **variable** deposit pattern, and variability reads as instability in an automated assessment — even when your total income went up.

If you are working while on claim and plan to borrow, expect to explain the pattern, and have both your pay stubs and your EI statements ready. Our guide on [preparing for a loan application](/resources/guides/preparing-for-loan-application) covers what to assemble.

## Fact 6: What lenders count for loans on maternity leave EI Canada applications

| Income source | Counted? | Practical note |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-leave salary | Yes, with confirmed return within ~12 months | Requires the return-to-work letter |
| EI maternity/parental | Yes, as temporary | Capped at $729/week standard, ~$438 extended |
| Employer SUB top-up | Often | Provide the plan documents |
| Canada Child Benefit | Sometimes, commonly at ~75% | $679.75/month maximum under age 6 as of July 2026 |
| Spousal income | Yes, on a joint application | Usually the single strongest move available |
| Part-time earnings while on claim | Yes, averaged | Bring stubs plus EI statements |

If there is a second earner in the household, a **joint application** will almost always outperform an attempt to qualify on benefit income alone — and it is the most reliable way to move loans on maternity leave EI Canada offers out of subprime pricing.

## Fact 7: Structure the loan around your return date

Three rules that keep the total cost down:

**Borrow for something finite.** A daycare deposit, a car repair, a move, a tax balance. Borrowing to cover general monthly shortfall for a year turns a $3,000 need into a five-figure debt.

**Match the term to the return.** If you return in month 12, a 36-month term means you are still paying for your leave two years later. Where you can sustain it, finish within 12–18 months of returning.

**Take the shortest term you can carry.** On $2,500 at 34.99%:

| Term | Monthly payment | Total interest |
|---|---|---|
| 12 months | ~$250 | ~$500 |
| 24 months | ~$147 | ~$1,040 |
| 36 months | ~$115 | ~$1,640 |

Moving from 12 to 36 months lowers the payment by about $135 and raises the cost by roughly **$1,140**. Always confirm there is no prepayment penalty so you can accelerate once you are back on salary.

## The month-13 problem nobody plans for

Almost every financial plan for a leave stops at the return-to-work date. The month *after* that date is consistently the hardest one, and it is worth building for now rather than discovering later.

Three costs land at once when you go back:

**Childcare begins.** Licensed care under CWELCC averages roughly **$435/month**, but unsubsidized infant care in major cities still runs well over $1,600. Either way it is a new line that did not exist during leave.

**The tax balance comes due.** If EI was under-withheld — and it often is in a year that mixes EI with employment income — the balance is payable by the end of April, which for many parents falls within weeks of returning.

**Salary lags by a pay cycle.** You return to work, but the first full paycheque arrives two to four weeks later. Meanwhile EI has already stopped.

That combination is the single most common reason a family that managed the entire leave without credit takes out **loans on maternity leave EI Canada** lenders classify as post-leave borrowing, at exactly the moment they thought they were finished.

The fix is a two-month bridge, built during the leave rather than after it. Set aside 5–10% of each EI deposit from day one. It covers the tax balance if there is one, the childcare deposit if there is one, and the pay-cycle gap regardless. On a $729 weekly benefit, 7% is about $51 a fortnight — roughly $1,300 across a standard leave, which is close to exactly what the month-13 gap costs.

## Before you sign: four questions

1. **"Can I set the payment date to two days after my EI deposit?"**
2. **"Is there a prepayment penalty if I pay this off when I return to work?"**
3. **"What is your hardship or deferral process if my return date moves?"**
4. **"Do you report to Equifax, TransUnion, or both?"** — without reporting, you pay subprime pricing with no credit benefit.

## Key takeaways

- **Loans on maternity leave EI Canada** approvals are sized against a **$729/week** ceiling — about $438 on the extended option.
- The **bi-weekly** EI cycle, not the amount, causes most missed payments. Set due dates two days after a deposit.
- The **return-to-work letter** can shift underwriting to your full pre-leave salary.
- EI is taxable and often **under-withheld** — set aside 5–10% of each deposit for April.
- Working while on claim reduces benefits by **50 cents on the dollar** up to 90% of your claim earnings, and creates a variable deposit pattern lenders scrutinize.
- Choose the shortest sustainable term and confirm there is **no prepayment penalty**.
