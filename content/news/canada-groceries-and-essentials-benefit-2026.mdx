The Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit is the federal government's new name for the **GST/HST credit**, and for most low-income households the practical change is the label — plus a welcome raise. As of July 2026, the **Canada Revenue Agency (CRA)** pays it quarterly; the first payment under the new name landed on **July 3, 2026**, alongside a **25% increase** to the benefit amount, and the last payment of the year is **October 5, 2026**. The benefit period runs **July 2026 to June 2027**, with the amount set by your **2025 tax return**. Here is exactly what the rebrand changes, what it doesn't, and how a modest quarterly cheque fits a stretched budget.

![Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit — a shopper checking a grocery receipt against a tight monthly budget](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4386326/pexels-photo-4386326.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## What the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit Actually Changes

Start with the reassuring part: the rename does **not** change how the payment works. It is the same tax-free, income-tested benefit the CRA has paid for years, now carrying a name that says plainly what it is for — helping with groceries and everyday essentials. You still qualify by filing a tax return, the CRA still calculates the amount automatically, and it still arrives four times a year.

What the rebrand *does* do is make the benefit easier to recognize and talk about. For households that never quite connected "GST/HST credit" to their grocery budget, a clearer name may mean fewer missed or misunderstood payments. Here is the benefit at a glance.

| Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit | Detail |
|---|---|
| Previous name | GST/HST credit |
| Who pays it | Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) |
| Payment frequency | Quarterly (four times a year) |
| 2026 payment dates | July 3 and October 5, 2026 |
| July 2026 change | Benefit amount raised 25% (through 2031) |
| Benefit period | July 2026 – June 2027 |
| Amount based on | Your 2025 tax return |
| Recent one-time top-up | Issued June 5, 2026 |
| If a payment is late | Wait 5 working days before contacting CRA |

## The 2026 Payment Dates — and July's 25% Boost

A few things stand out this year. First, July 2026 brought a **25% increase** to the benefit amount — a boost Ottawa says will hold from 2026 through 2031. Second, the first payment under the new name arrived **July 3, 2026**, and the **last quarterly payment of the year is October 5, 2026**. Separately, a **one-time GST/HST credit top-up was issued on June 5, 2026** — an extra one-off that some households already received, not part of the regular schedule.

If a payment does not show up, don't panic and don't call right away. The CRA asks you to **wait 5 working days** before contacting them, because processing and direct-deposit timing can vary by a few days. Making sure your **direct deposit and mailing address are current** in CRA My Account is the single best way to avoid a delay.

## How a Modest Benefit Fits a Tight Budget

Be realistic about scale. Under the previous GST/HST credit branding, the maximum for the July 2025–June 2026 year was **up to $533 for a single adult and up to $698 for a couple**, according to Global News — spread across four payments. Your benefit for the current July 2026–June 2027 period is calculated from your 2025 return, so amounts vary, but for most recipients this is a helpful supplement, not a household income.

That is exactly why it pays to plan around it rather than count on it:

- **Earmark it.** Because it lands on a known date, assign each payment to a specific recurring cost — groceries, a utility bill, a transit pass — so it does real work instead of disappearing into general spending. A simple [budget tool](/resources/tools) makes that easy.
- **Protect it from interest.** A quarterly benefit is quickly cancelled out if high-interest debt is eating your paycheque. If credit-card balances are the pressure point, consolidating them into one lower-rate [debt consolidation loan](/loans/by-type/debt-consolidation-loans) can free up more room than the benefit itself.
- **Know your capacity.** Before taking on any new payment, check your [debt-to-income ratio](/resources/guides/understanding-debt-to-income-ratio) so you borrow only what your budget can genuinely carry.

## The Bottom Line

The Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit is a rebrand with substance mostly in the name: the same CRA-paid, income-tested quarterly benefit, now easier to recognize and **25% larger**, with its first payment under the new name issued July 3, 2026 and the last of the year on **October 5, 2026**, amounts set by your 2025 return. File your taxes, keep your CRA details current, and give each payment a job. And if debt — not just the price of groceries — is what's squeezing the budget, [comparing personal loan options](/loans) or consolidating high-interest balances may do more than any single benefit cheque.
