**Canada home sales july 2026** rose for a fourth consecutive month, according to figures the Canadian Real Estate Association released on **August 18, 2026**. It is a genuine run of gains. It is also, measured against last year, still a contraction — sales remain **5.3% below** July 2025.

![Canada home sales july 2026 — reviewing mortgage and purchase figures before buying a home](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4098152/pexels-photo-4098152.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## Summary

- Sales **+0.5% month over month** — the **fourth** straight monthly rise
- Sales **−5.3% year over year**
- National average price **$674,819**, **+0.2% y/y**
- MLS Home Price Index **−3.3% y/y**, but **+0.1% m/m** — first monthly gain since November 2024
- New listings **−1.6% m/m**, a third consecutive drop
- Months of inventory **4.7**, the lowest of 2026
- Next CREA release: **September 15, 2026**

## Canada home sales july 2026: two true statements

Both of the following are accurate, and the gap between them explains most of the confusing coverage this month.

**Sales are recovering.** Four consecutive monthly increases is a real trend, and CREA chair **Garry Bhaura** said "more moderate housing market conditions can be expected to continue to bring buyers off the sidelines going forward."

**Sales are still weak.** The monthly gains started from a low base. The July total remains **5.3% below** the same month last year, so the market has spent 2026 climbing back toward where it was rather than beyond it.

Month-over-month numbers describe momentum. Year-over-year numbers describe level. Canada home sales july 2026 data shows improving momentum at a still-depressed level.

But prices have not risen enough to help sellers either — the quality-adjusted index is still down 3.3% from a year ago.

CREA senior economist **Shaun Cathcart** described the underlying dynamic as markets "generally moving back towards balance."

## Background: average price versus price index

In the canada home sales july 2026 release these two figures moved in opposite directions, and July is a good example — the average price was up **0.2%** while the MLS Home Price Index was down **3.3%**.

The **average price** is simply the mean of what sold. If more expensive properties make up a larger share of sales in a given month, the average rises even if no individual home gained value.

The **MLS Home Price Index** adjusts for the type and quality of homes sold, tracking what a comparable property is worth over time. For judging whether prices are actually rising or falling, the index is the more reliable of the two.

On that measure, prices are still lower than a year ago — though July delivered the first monthly increase since **November 2024**.

Regionally, CREA noted Saskatchewan, New Brunswick and Newfoundland and Labrador remain borderline sellers' markets, with most other provinces converging toward long-term averages.

## Why this matters

**For buyers:** the canada home sales july 2026 figures suggest the period of maximum negotiating leverage is passing. Fewer listings and falling inventory usually precede firmer prices. Anyone waiting for a materially better entry point is now betting against a supply trend that has run three months.

**For owners and renewers:** a flat-to-slightly-positive month arrests the decline in equity, which matters most for households facing a renewal where the appraised value affects the loan-to-value ratio. Canada's mortgage renewal wave continues through 2026.

**For borrowing costs:** the Bank of Canada meets **September 2, 2026**. July headline inflation was **3.0%**, at the top of the Bank's 1%–3% control range, but its preferred core measures averaged **2.0%** and inflation excluding gasoline was **2.2%** for a third straight month. Shelter inflation specifically was just **1.3%**.

## Canada home sales july 2026: key takeaways

1. **Four consecutive monthly sales gains** — but sales are still **5.3% below** last year.
2. **The average price of $674,819 is up 0.2%**, while the quality-adjusted index is **down 3.3%**.
3. **Inventory at 4.7 months is the lowest of 2026**, reducing buyer leverage.
4. **Listings have fallen three months running** — supply, not demand, is driving the shift.
5. **September 2** is the next Bank of Canada rate decision; **September 15** the next CREA report.

## Conclusion

July was not a turning point for affordability. Prices remain below year-ago levels and the average sale price is flat in nominal terms, which is a decline once July's 3.0% inflation is applied.

What did change is the balance of options. With inventory at its lowest level this year and listings still withdrawing, buyers have less room to wait than they did in the spring — without yet having gained much on price.
