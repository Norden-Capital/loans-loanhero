**Canada insolvency filings 2026** totalled **145,762 consumer cases** in the twelve months ending 30 June, according to data released by the Office of the Superintendent of Bankruptcy (OSB) on 10 August. Quarterly headlines have focused on the second quarter being the heaviest since 2009 — but the rolling twelve-month view answers a more useful question: is this a spike still building, or a plateau at an uncomfortable height?

## Summary

- **145,762 consumer insolvency filings** in the 12 months to 30 June 2026
- **4,743 business filings** over the same period
- **77,658 total filings** under the *Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act* in the first half of 2026
- Q2 consumer filings of **37,523** — highest since 2009, but only **+1.1%** on Q1

## Canada Insolvency Filings 2026: The Twelve-Month View

Quarterly reporting invites a fresh "highest since 2009" headline every three months. The rolling annual figure strips that out.

| Measure | 12 months to 30 June 2026 |
|---|---|
| Consumer filings | **145,762** |
| Business filings | 4,743 |
| First-half 2026 total (BIA) | 77,658 |

At roughly 12,100 consumer filings a month on average, the Canada insolvency filings 2026 run-rate is elevated and remarkably steady. That steadiness is itself the finding — the quarterly totals of 37,121 in Q1 and 37,523 in Q2 differ by about one percent.

## Spike or Plateau? The Honest Answer

There is a genuine analytical disagreement about the Canada insolvency filings 2026 trend, and it is worth presenting rather than resolving artificially.

**The case for a plateau.** The quarter-over-quarter increase was 1.1%. Charles St-Arnaud, chief economist at Servus Credit Union, told BNN Bloomberg that insolvency rates "appear to be stabilizing near levels last seen in 2009," while noting that "high household debt and stagnating purchasing power have put pressure on Canadians' finances in recent years." A flat quarter after several rising ones is consistent with a peak forming.

**The case for a continuing climb.** Year over year, consumer filings are still up 6.9%. June alone came in at 13,254 filings, up 11.5% from a year earlier and 5.7% from May — a stronger finish than the quarter's average. And the composition shifted, with bankruptcies rising about 13.5% on the quarter while proposals fell about 2.1%.

The reasonable conclusion is that the **level** has plateaued while the **severity** has not obviously done so. Those are different measurements, and conflating them produces the contradictory headlines readers have seen over the past week.

## Background: What Sits Behind the Numbers

The Canada insolvency filings 2026 totals cap a cycle that has run at post-financial-crisis highs throughout, driven by the accumulated effect of housing costs, several years of above-target inflation, and interest rate increases still working through renewals and revolving credit.

The Bank of Canada's policy rate has been held at 2.25% since July, but a hold does not unwind debt already carried at higher cost. Provincially, the pressure is uneven: Ontario recorded 15,029 filings in Q2, up 10.0% year over year, while Quebec recorded 9,361, up 3.0%. ## Canada Insolvency Filings 2026: The Recovery Side

A statistic about 145,762 filings is also a statistic about 145,762 processes that **end**. That is the part rarely covered, and it is the part that matters most to anyone in the middle of one.

- A **consumer proposal** typically stays on a credit report for about three years after completion.
- A **first bankruptcy** typically stays for six to seven years after discharge.
- **Rebuilding usually begins within months**, not years, of completing the process.

The practical sequence afterwards is unglamorous and effective: a secured card or a small loan that reports to the credit bureaus, used lightly and paid on time. Payment history is the single largest factor in a credit score, and consistency over six to twelve months does more than the size of any individual account.

One caution: taking on new borrowing **during** an active proposal should always be discussed with your Licensed Insolvency Trustee first, because debt that jeopardises the agreed payments can put the entire arrangement at risk.

## Key Takeaways

- **145,762 consumer filings** in the twelve months to 30 June 2026
- The quarterly **level has flattened** — Q1 and Q2 differ by about 1%
- The **severity has not**, with bankruptcies rising and proposals falling
- Year-over-year growth remains **6.9%**, so this is a high plateau, not a decline
- Every filing is a finite process; **recovery timelines are measured in years, not decades**

## Conclusion

The Canada insolvency filings 2026 data describes a country whose household insolvency rate has stopped climbing steeply but has settled at its highest sustained level since the financial crisis. For readers, the two figures worth carrying forward are the annual total of 145,762 and the fact that Q1 and Q2 were within a percentage point of each other. The trend has levelled; it has not turned. And for anyone inside those numbers, the process has a defined end and a well-worn path back.
