The most reliable **Canada interest rate forecast 2026** comes not from pundits but from the Bank of Canada's own Market Participants Survey — and the professionals who trade rates for a living are clear: no cuts are on the way. The survey's median respondent sees the policy rate holding at **2.25%** for the rest of 2026, then rising — not falling — through 2027. For anyone waiting on cheaper money before tackling high-interest debt, that is the headline. This is a separate signal from the Bank's July 15 hold: it tells you where the market thinks rates go *next*.

![Canada interest rate forecast 2026 illustrated by an analyst reviewing Bank of Canada policy-rate charts and projections](https://images.pexels.com/photos/4386366/pexels-photo-4386366.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## What the Canada Interest Rate Forecast 2026 Actually Says

The Bank of Canada ran its second-quarter Market Participants Survey from **June 11 to 18, 2026**, gathering the views of about **26 financial-market participants** — banks, primary dealers, pension funds, insurers and asset managers — on the policy rate and the economy for Canada and the U.S. On rates, the median path is flat, then upward:

| Period | Median expected policy rate |
|---|---|
| End of 2026 | 2.25% (unchanged) |
| First quarter of 2027 | 2.50% |
| Second half of 2027 | 2.75% |
| Through 2028 | 2.75% |

*Source: Bank of Canada, Market Participants Survey — Second Quarter of 2026 (median responses).*

In plain terms, the median participant expects the Bank to sit at 2.25% through December 2026, lift the rate to **2.50%** by around March 2027, and reach **2.75%** in the second half of 2027 — then hold there into 2028. That 2.75% endpoint is no accident: it matches the survey's median estimate of Canada's **long-run neutral rate**, the level seen as neither stimulating nor restraining growth.

## Why the Pros See Hikes, Not Cuts

A forecast of higher rates only makes sense against the inflation backdrop. Survey respondents put median headline CPI inflation at **2.6%** for the end of 2026 — still above the Bank's 2% target — easing to **2.1%** by the end of 2027. With growth soft (a median of just **1.3%** real GDP growth for late 2026) but inflation sticky, the market reads the Bank as having little room to ease. Respondents even pegged the median probability of a recession in the next 6-to-12 months at only **25%** — low enough that emergency rate relief looks unlikely. That is the core of the **Canada interest rate forecast 2026** that markets are now pricing.

It lines up with the Bank's actual behaviour. On July 15, 2026, the Bank of Canada [held its policy rate at 2.25%](/news/bank-of-canada-rate-decision-july-2026), and the survey suggests markets expect that hold to persist before the next move is *up*. The professional consensus, in a phrase: higher for longer, then a little higher still.

## What the Forecast Means for Borrowers

Here is the practical read. If the median **Canada interest rate forecast 2026** is right, waiting for the Bank to rescue your budget with a rate cut is a losing bet — there is no cut to wait for, and the next scheduled direction is higher.

![A borrower weighing whether to consolidate high-interest debt now or wait, guided by Canada's 2026 interest-rate outlook](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6693661/pexels-photo-6693661.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

For anyone carrying credit-card balances at 20% or more, the takeaway is timing. A held-then-rising policy rate means the cost of variable, high-interest debt is not about to fall on its own. The most effective lever is usually not the Bank of Canada — it is reducing the interest you pay *now*. Rolling several high-rate balances into one lower-rate [debt consolidation loan](/loans/by-type/debt-consolidation-loans) replaces multiple payments with a single, predictable one, and frees up cash flow this month rather than in some hypothetical easier future.

Two things matter more than the forecast:

1. **A fixed-rate loan doesn't move with the Bank.** Once you sign a fixed-rate [personal loan](/loans/by-type/personal-loans), your interest rate and payment are locked for the full term — any 2027 hikes won't touch them.
2. **Your credit profile drives your rate more than the overnight rate does.** Lenders price you on your credit score, income stability and debt load. The median **Canada interest rate forecast 2026** from the Bank of Canada's Market Participants Survey is a hold at 2.25% now, followed by gradual increases toward a 2.75% neutral rate by late 2027.
