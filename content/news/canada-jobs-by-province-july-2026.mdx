Canada jobs by province July 2026 reveal a national headline built almost entirely in one place. Employment rose **75,000** across the country — and **52,000 of that came from Ontario alone**, roughly **69%** of the total. Quebec and Alberta, meanwhile, were little changed. A strong national number described a very uneven month.

## Canada jobs by province July 2026: the breakdown

Statistics Canada's Labour Force Survey covers the reference week of **July 12 to 18, 2026**.

| Province | Change | Percent | Share of national gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ontario** | **+52,000** | +0.6% | **~69%** |
| British Columbia | +18,000 | +0.6% | ~24% |
| Manitoba | +5,900 | **+0.8%** | ~8% |
| Nova Scotia | +4,600 | **+0.9%** | ~6% |
| Quebec | Little changed | — | — |
| Alberta | Little changed | — | — |
| **Canada** | **+75,000** | +0.4% | 100% |

The listed provincial gains sum to more than the national total, which is normal — other provinces and territories saw offsetting movements, and Statistics Canada reports only the changes it considers statistically notable.

## What Canada jobs by province July 2026 tells us

**Ontario carried the month.** A 52,000 increase in a single province is the story of this release. At +0.6%, its growth rate matched British Columbia's, but its size meant it dominated the national figure.

**Quebec and Alberta stood still.** These are Canada's second and fourth most populous provinces. Employment being "little changed" in both, in a month the country added 75,000 jobs, is the most underreported line in the release.

**The fastest growth was in the smallest provinces.** Nova Scotia (**+0.9%**) and Manitoba (**+0.8%**) posted higher percentage gains than either Ontario or British Columbia. In absolute terms those are modest numbers — 4,600 and 5,900 — but relative to their labour forces they outgrew the big provinces.

## Why the regional split matters for borrowing

The reason Canada jobs by province July 2026 is worth reading past the headline is simple: the national unemployment rate is the number that makes news, and it is rarely the number that describes your situation.

Three reasons the provincial view is more useful:

1. **Lending conditions track regional employment.** Lenders watch delinquency and default patterns, and those are regional. A province with flat employment and one adding jobs at 0.6% do not present the same risk picture.
2. **Your own job security is local.** A borrower in Alberta reading "unemployment hits two-year low" had a month in which their provincial employment did not move.
3. **Housing and rent costs follow regional labour demand.** Job growth concentrated in Ontario tends to feed through into Ontario housing pressure, which shapes what borrowers there can afford.

## The national picture around it

For context on the numbers the provincial data sits inside:

- Employment **+75,000 (+0.4%)**, split between full-time and part-time work.
- Unemployment rate **6.4%** — down 0.1 points on the month and **0.5 points year over year**, its lowest in two years.
- Employment rate **60.9%**, up 0.1 points.
- Average hourly wages **$37.17**, up **2.8%** year over year.

That wage figure is worth pairing with prices: the most recent inflation reading, June's CPI at **2.8%**, matches it. Pay is running level with costs rather than ahead of them — in every province.

## How concentrated is 69%, really?

Ontario holds roughly **38%** of Canada's population but produced about **69%** of July's employment gain. That is close to double its demographic share in a single month.

Concentration like that is not unusual in any one month — provincial figures are volatile and Statistics Canada reports only movements it considers notable. But it does mean the national number is describing Ontario more than it is describing Canada.

| Reading the report | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| National headline (+75,000) | The country's aggregate direction |
| Your province's line | Whether your local market moved at all |
| Your industry's line | Whether your own work is affected |

The useful habit with Canada jobs by province July 2026, or any month's release, is to read all three lines rather than the first one. A resident of Alberta reading only the headline would conclude their job market improved in July. Their provincial line says it was little changed.

## Key takeaways

- **Ontario added 52,000 jobs**, about **69%** of Canada's entire July gain.
- **British Columbia added 18,000**; both grew 0.6%.
- **Nova Scotia (+0.9%) and Manitoba (+0.8%)** posted the fastest percentage growth.
- **Quebec and Alberta were little changed** despite the strong national figure.
- **National unemployment fell to 6.4%**, a two-year low; wages rose 2.8% to $37.17.

## The bottom line

Canada jobs by province July 2026 is a reminder that national statistics are averages, and averages hide their own composition. The country added 75,000 jobs; two of its four largest provinces added essentially none.

For borrowers, the practical read is to weight your own province's line above the national headline. A two-year low in unemployment is real and welcome — but whether it describes your job market depends entirely on where you live.
