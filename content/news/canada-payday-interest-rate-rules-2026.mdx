If you have bad credit and few options, the most valuable thing you can know before borrowing in 2026 is the ceiling the law puts on what a lender can charge you. **Canada's rules on payday loans and loan interest** were tightened at the start of 2025, and they are the quiet backstop protecting borrowers who are most often targeted by high-cost offers: a **35% APR cap** on regular loans, and a **$14-per-$100** cap on payday loans. With insolvencies climbing and more households leaning on expensive credit, it is worth understanding exactly what these rules do — and where their limits leave you exposed.

## The two caps that protect you

Canada actually has two separate rules, and knowing which applies to your loan is the key to spotting an illegal offer.

| Rule (in force in 2026) | The cap | Applies to |
|---|---|---|
| Criminal rate of interest | 35% APR (effective annual rate) | Regular personal and installment loans |
| Payday loan cost cap | $14 per $100 borrowed | Small short-term loans (typically under $1,500, 62 days or less) |

The **35% criminal interest rate** took effect January 1, 2025, replacing the old 60% ceiling. Any standard loan charging more than 35% APR is a criminal offence — full stop. Payday loans sit in a separate lane: they are exempt from the 35% cap but are limited to **$14 in fees per $100 borrowed**. That is the rule that governs the storefront and online "get cash today" products.

## Why payday loans are still expensive

Here is the catch the $14 figure hides. Fourteen dollars on a hundred sounds modest, but a payday loan is repaid in a couple of weeks, not a year. Annualize that short, high-fee loan and it works out to roughly **350%** — far above the 35% cap that governs a normal loan. The cap makes payday loans *less* punishing than they used to be, but it does not make them cheap, and rolling one over repeatedly is how a small shortfall becomes a spiral.

That is why the rules are a floor of protection, not a green light. A legal payday loan is still one of the most expensive ways to borrow, and for anything beyond a genuine two-week gap there is almost always a cheaper route. ## How to use the rules to protect yourself

Think of the caps as a simple test you can apply to any offer.

- **On a regular loan, demand the APR.** If it works out above 35%, it is illegal — walk away and report it.
- **On a small short-term loan, do the fee math.** More than $14 per $100 borrowed is outside the rules.
- **Watch for structures that dodge the caps.** "Membership fees," "insurance," or add-ons that push the true cost higher are exactly what predatory lenders use. Our guide on [spotting predatory lenders](/resources/guides/how-to-spot-predatory-lenders) shows the tactics.
- **Never pay an upfront fee to "release" a loan.** That is an advance-fee scam, not a legal cost.

If you are borrowing because money is tight, the goal is to pay the least to solve the problem — and to avoid a product that makes next month worse. A regulated [payday-alternative loan](/loans/by-type/payday-alternative-loans) or a small installment loan repaid over several months usually costs a fraction of repeated payday borrowing, and one that reports to the credit bureaus helps your file recover while you repay.

### Provinces add their own payday rules

The federal $14-per-$100 figure is not the whole story, because payday lending is also regulated province by province. Most provinces set their own maximum cost of borrowing, licensing requirements, and rules on rollovers and repeat loans — and the exact fee cap can differ from one province to the next. Before you borrow, check your province's rules, which a licensed lender must display, and be doubly wary of any online operation that ignores provincial licensing altogether. Operating without a provincial licence is a common marker of an illegal lender.

## The bottom line

Canada's 2026 lending rules give every borrower a clear line to stand behind: 35% APR on a regular loan, $14 per $100 on a payday loan, and anything beyond that is illegal. Those caps matter most for bad-credit borrowers, who are the likeliest targets for high-cost and predatory offers. Use them as a test on every quote, remember that a legal payday loan is still expensive, and reach for the cheaper, credit-building alternative whenever you can.
