The **CREA housing forecast 2026** was revised on **July 15, 2026**, and the headline is stability rather than drama: the Canadian Real Estate Association now expects **463,336** residential properties to change hands through Canadian MLS Systems this year — a **1.4% decline** from 2025 — with the national average price rising **1.1% to $686,710**. Both are small adjustments to CREA's April numbers, and the revision was driven by specific regions rather than a national turn.

*Reported August 2, 2026, based on the Canadian Real Estate Association's forecast update of July 15, 2026. Forecasts are estimates and are revised quarterly. This is news reporting and general information, not financial advice.*

## The 5 key numbers

| Measure | 2026 forecast |
|---|---:|
| MLS residential sales | **463,336** |
| Change from 2025 | **−1.4%** |
| National average price | **$686,710** |
| Change from 2025 | **+1.1%** |
| June 2026 monthly sales | +0.5% (after +5.5% in May) |

## What the CREA housing forecast 2026 revision actually says

The forecast contains a tension worth unpacking, because it is easy to misread.

**The annual number is down. The monthly trend is up.** Sales rose 0.9% in April, jumped 5.5% in May, and edged up a further 0.5% in June — leaving national activity roughly **7% above** where it stood in March. The forecast decline of 1.4% is measured against the *full year 2025*, and reflects how weak the opening months of 2026 were, not where the market is heading right now.

**The revision was regional, not national.** CREA attributed the downgrade to a faster-than-expected slowdown in parts of Canada facing two headwinds simultaneously: a sharp reduction in population growth, and historic supply shortages. It named **Quebec and the East Coast**. Those are markets that ran hot on interprovincial migration and now face the reversal.

**Prices are still forecast to rise.** A 1.1% national increase is barely above flat in real terms, but it is not a decline. Anyone waiting for a national price correction is waiting for something this forecast does not project.

## Background: the affordability picture behind the CREA housing forecast 2026

Affordability improved to its **best reading in four years** in the first quarter of 2026 — a genuine improvement, and one that needs context.

The improvement came primarily from the interest rate side. The Bank of Canada cut aggressively through the 2025 trade-related slowdown and has held its policy rate at **2.25%** through six consecutive decisions, most recently on **July 15, 2026**, keeping prime at **4.45%**.

But a lower policy rate does not translate directly into an affordable purchase. Buyers still qualify at current mortgage rates rather than the policy rate, still carry large loan balances relative to income, and still face elevated everyday costs. "Best in four years" measures improvement from a very difficult starting point.

## Why this matters for buyers and owners

A forecast of flat sales and a 1.1% price increase is, for most households, a description of a market that has stopped moving against them without moving in their favour either.

Four practical implications:

- **Waiting is no longer an obvious strategy.** When prices were falling, delay had a payoff. A forecast of modest increases removes it, and carrying costs while you wait are real.
- **National averages are close to useless for a personal decision.** The same forecast that shows +1.1% nationally contains regional slowdowns severe enough to move the national number. Quebec and Atlantic Canada are behaving differently from the national figure.
- **The rate tailwind is probably over.** The Bank of Canada's own Market Participants Survey for the second quarter of 2026 shows the median respondent expecting no further cuts in 2026 and a first increase in March 2027. Buyers should not build a purchase around further rate relief.
- **Renewals continue regardless.** Roughly 1.15 million mortgages come up for renewal in 2026, and Bank of Canada data has indicated about 60% of borrowers renewing across 2025 and 2026 face higher payments. That pressure is independent of what sales volumes do.

For owners not transacting, the **CREA housing forecast 2026** is mostly background. For anyone buying, selling or renewing in the next twelve months, the more useful numbers are local sales-to-new-listings ratios and your own qualifying payment — not the national average.

## Key takeaways

- The **CREA housing forecast 2026** projects **463,336** MLS sales, down **1.4%**, with the average price up **1.1% to $686,710**.
- The downgrade was driven by **Quebec and the East Coast**, not a national turn.
- Monthly sales have risen for three consecutive months and sit about **7% above March**.
- Affordability hit a **four-year best** in Q1 2026, largely because of lower policy rates.
- Prices are forecast to **rise modestly**, so waiting for a national decline is not supported by this forecast.

## What to watch next

CREA updates the CREA housing forecast 2026 quarterly, so the next revision will show whether the spring momentum carried into the second half. Watch monthly sales-to-new-listings ratios in your own market — they turn well before average prices do.
