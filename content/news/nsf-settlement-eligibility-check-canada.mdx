**NSF settlement eligibility** is decided by bank records rather than by anything you submit — which is why thousands of Canadians have received small unexplained deposits this summer without knowing why. Four of Canada's five largest banks have now settled claims over duplicate non-sufficient-funds fees for a combined **$43.4 million**, and payments run automatically. Here is how to work out whether any of it belongs to you.

*Published August 4, 2026. General information, not legal advice. Eligibility is determined by the courts and class counsel.*

![Reviewing bank statements to confirm NSF settlement eligibility in Canada](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6538437/pexels-photo-6538437.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## The fee pattern NSF settlement eligibility turns on

All five actions, brought by Toronto firm Koskie Minsky LLP, allege the same thing.

A biller presents a pre-authorized debit. The account is short, the payment fails, an NSF fee is charged. The biller's system automatically presents **the same payment again** days later. It fails again — and a second fee lands.

The claims argued this penalised customers twice for one failed payment, breaching account contracts and consumer protection law. **No bank admitted liability** in any settlement.

So when you test your own NSF settlement eligibility, you are not looking for a big fee. You are looking for **two fees, close together, for the same thing**.

## The 7 NSF settlement eligibility checks

### 1. Identify which bank held the account

NSF settlement eligibility is bank-specific. Each has its own fund and its own class period, and a duplicate fee at a bank with no settlement does not qualify anywhere.

### 2. Check the fee sat inside that bank's class period

| Bank | Class period | Fund |
|---|---|---|
| TD | Ended before Feb 2024 approval | $15.9M |
| **Scotiabank** | **Jun 21, 2020 – Apr 30, 2024** | $10.45M |
| **CIBC** | **Sep 21, 2020 – May 31, 2024** | $10M (proposed) |
| RBC | **Aug 30, 2020 – Aug 1, 2022** | $7.05M |
| BMO | Not defined — uncertified | — |

Dates matter more than people expect. RBC's window closed in **August 2022**, nearly two years earlier than Scotiabank's.

### 3. Look for two fees, 2 to 30 days apart

This is the defining test for NSF settlement eligibility. The published criteria for Scotiabank require a **$48 NSF fee** charged on a re-presented pre-authorized debit **between two and 30 days** after a previous fee. RBC's used the same structure at **$45**.

A single NSF fee, however painful, does not qualify.

### 4. Confirm the two charges came from the same merchant, same amount

The claims target one payment being retried, not two separate payments failing. Scotiabank's criteria specify the same merchant with **matching transaction amounts**.

Scan your statement for a repeated biller name and an identical dollar figure within that window.

### 5. Check whether the account is still open

This is where NSF settlement eligibility quietly breaks down for most people who otherwise qualify.

These settlements pay by **direct deposit into an account at that bank**. Scotiabank's criteria require the account to be open and able to accept deposits at the distribution date. If you have since switched banks, you may satisfy every other condition and still receive nothing automatically.

**Do not treat that as the end of it** — contact class counsel, who administer distribution.

### 6. Check the right notification channel

Scotiabank notified eligible class members through **Scotia Online and its mobile app** from around **July 13, 2026** — not by mail. Anyone watching their letterbox saw nothing.

If you rarely open your banking app's message centre, that is the first place to look.

### 7. Confirm the settlement is actually approved

NSF settlement eligibility means nothing until a court has signed off.

| Bank | Status as of August 2026 |
|---|---|
| TD | Approved Feb 15, 2024 — **paid, concluded** |
| RBC | Approved Sep 16, 2025 — **paid, concluded** |
| **Scotiabank** | Approved Jun 12, 2026 — **distributing now** |
| CIBC | **Hearing Oct 19, 2026** — nothing paid yet |
| BMO | **Uncertified** — no settlement exists |

CIBC customers in particular should be careful here. The $10 million figure is real, but it is a **proposal**. No money moves unless a judge approves it in October.

## What NSF settlement eligibility is actually worth

Per-person amounts have varied widely, because fund sizes and class sizes differ:

- **TD** — about **$88** per eligible account, the highest
- **Scotiabank** — about **$42.82**
- **RBC** — about **$25**
- **CIBC** — not published
- **BMO** — none

Set against the fees themselves, the refunds are partial. NSF charges at the Big Six ran **$45 to $48** through the class periods, a weighted average near **$46.85**. A payment that bounced twice could cost close to **$96** — on a debit that might have been for $8.

## The scam that follows NSF settlement eligibility news

Automatic payouts attract fraud with grim reliability. In 2025 the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre logged more than **112,000 reports** and over **$704 million** in reported losses — the highest on record — and only an estimated 5% to 10% of fraud is ever reported.

Three rules cover this entirely:

- **You never pay to receive a class action settlement.** No filing fee, no processing fee, no "release" fee.
- **Class counsel never asks for your online banking password.** The bank already holds your account details; that is the whole mechanism.
- **Verify through the law firm's own website or your banking app**, never through a link in a text or email.

## What changed for the future

The practice behind these lawsuits has already been addressed by regulation, separately from the courts.

Since **March 12, 2026**, amendments to the Financial Consumer Protection Framework Regulations cap NSF fees at **$10** at federally regulated banks, prohibit **more than one NSF fee within two business days** on the same account, and bar any fee where the overdraft is under **$10**.

That middle rule is the direct answer to the re-presentment sequence. The lawsuits refund the past; the cap constrains what can happen next time.

## Key takeaways

- **NSF settlement eligibility is automatic** — determined from bank records, with no form to file and no fee.
- Look for **two NSF fees, 2 to 30 days apart, same biller, same amount**, inside the class period.
- **Scotiabank is paying now** (~$42.82); **CIBC awaits an Oct 19, 2026 hearing**; **BMO has no settlement**.
- **TD (~$88)** and **RBC (~$25)** are concluded.
- **A closed account** is the most common reason an eligible person receives nothing — contact class counsel.
- **Nobody legitimate charges a fee** to process these payments.

## The bottom line

If you ran a tight balance at any point between 2020 and 2024, spend ten minutes with your old statements looking for the same biller failing twice inside a month. That single pattern is what decides NSF settlement eligibility, and for Scotiabank customers the money is moving right now. Everything else — the fund size, the court dates, the per-person figure — follows from it.

For everything after March 2026, the more useful protection is the cap itself: $10, once per two business days, no fee at all under a $10 overdraft.
