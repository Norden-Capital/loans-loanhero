The **OSFI credit risk guideline** consultation closed on **July 29, 2026**, marking the first formal step toward folding Canada's best-known mortgage rulebook — Guideline **B-20** — into a single consolidated framework. The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions is building one principle-based guideline covering mortgage lending, commercial real estate and corporate lending, to be issued in draft segments across 2026 and 2027.

*Reported August 2, 2026, based on OSFI's consultative document on Credit Risk Management and related published guidance. Draft chapters have not yet been released and details may change. This is news reporting and general information, not financial or legal advice.*

![Mortgage underwriting documents being reviewed as the OSFI credit risk guideline consultation closes](https://images.pexels.com/photos/6693661/pexels-photo-6693661.jpeg?auto=compress&cs=tinysrgb&w=1200)

## The 6 key changes ahead

| # | Change | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | One consolidated guideline | Mortgage, commercial real estate and corporate lending expectations merge into a single document |
| 2 | B-20 absorbed | Guideline B-20 and its related advisories, notices and letters move into a real estate secured lending chapter |
| 3 | Principle-based approach | Fewer prescriptive rules, more expectations about how institutions manage risk |
| 4 | Full loan lifecycle focus | Emphasis extends beyond origination to ongoing monitoring and management |
| 5 | Phased consultation | Draft segments released progressively through 2026 and 2027 |
| 6 | International alignment | Incorporates international best practice on credit risk |

## What the OSFI credit risk guideline does — and does not — change today

This is the point most worth being precise about, because consolidation of mortgage guidance is easily misread as a change to mortgage qualification.

**Nothing has changed for borrowers yet.** OSFI has indicated that Guideline B-20 continues to drive underwriting expectations for residential lending while this work proceeds. **No change to the minimum qualifying rate — the mortgage stress test — has been announced** as part of the consultation.

What the OSFI credit risk guideline does at this stage is restructure how expectations are organised and where the supervisory emphasis sits. Today's framework grew by accretion: B-20 for residential mortgages, separate guidance for commercial real estate and corporate exposures, and a long tail of advisories, notices and letters layered on top. The new approach replaces that with one document built around principles rather than a list of discrete rules.

The substantive shift is in **timing**. B-20 is fundamentally about underwriting at origination — how a lender decides whether to make the loan. OSFI has signalled a focus on credit risk management **throughout the lifecycle of a loan**, which means ongoing monitoring, portfolio-level oversight and management of exposures well after the mortgage closes.

## Background: why the OSFI credit risk guideline is being written now

OSFI has been running a broader program to modernize its policies, guidance and supervision for regulatory efficiency — reducing overlap and making expectations easier for institutions to apply consistently.

The context for the credit risk piece specifically is a Canadian lending environment that has been unusually eventful. Roughly **1.15 million mortgages** come up for renewal in 2026, and Bank of Canada data has indicated about **60%** of mortgage holders renewing across 2025 and 2026 face higher payments, with about three-quarters of that group holding five-year fixed-rate mortgages. Insolvency filings, meanwhile, reached their highest quarterly level since 2009 in the first quarter of 2026.

A supervisory framework oriented toward the whole life of a loan rather than the moment of approval is a reasonable response to a portfolio repricing at scale. That context explains the timing of the OSFI credit risk guideline more than any single event does.

### Timeline

| Date | Development |
|---|---|
| Early 2026 | OSFI signals a consolidated Credit Risk Management Guideline as part of its modernization program |
| Q1–Q2 2026 | Consultative document published; six-month consultation on overarching principles and key content areas |
| **July 29, 2026** | Consultation input deadline |
| 2026–2027 | Draft segments issued in phases, including a real estate secured lending chapter |

## Why this matters

For homeowners and buyers, the practical answer to the OSFI credit risk guideline today is: watch, do not act. The rules that determine whether you qualify have not moved, and the chapter that could change them has not been drafted.

Three groups should pay closer attention as draft chapters appear:

- **Borrowers renewing in 2026–2027.** Renewals with the same lender have generally not required requalification at the stress-test rate. How a lifecycle-oriented framework treats ongoing exposures is worth watching for anyone planning to switch lenders at renewal.
- **Self-employed and non-traditional income earners.** Principle-based guidance gives institutions more room to exercise judgement. That can widen access — or narrow it — depending on how individual lenders interpret the expectations.
- **Borrowers using alternative lenders.** The OSFI credit risk guideline binds federally regulated institutions, not provincial credit unions or private lenders. Historically, tightening at OSFI-regulated banks has pushed some borrowers toward the alternative channel, where pricing is higher.

It is also worth noting what the OSFI credit risk guideline does **not** touch. Consumer lending rates in Canada remain capped at **35% APR** under the criminal interest rate provisions in force since January 1, 2025, and licensed payday loans remain capped separately at $14 per $100. Those limits are set in federal law, not supervisory guidance, and are unaffected by this work.

## Key takeaways

- The **OSFI credit risk guideline** consultation closed **July 29, 2026**.
- It will consolidate mortgage, commercial real estate and corporate lending expectations into **one principle-based guideline**.
- **Guideline B-20** will be absorbed into a real estate secured lending chapter, along with related advisories and notices.
- **No change to the mortgage stress test has been announced**; B-20 continues to apply for now.
- Draft segments arrive in phases across **2026 and 2027**.
- The framework applies to **federally regulated** institutions, not provincial credit unions or private lenders.

## What to watch next

The signal to watch for is publication of the **real estate secured lending draft chapter**. That document, not the consultation that just closed, is where any borrower-facing change to residential mortgage expectations would first appear. Until it is published, speculation about stress-test changes is exactly that.
